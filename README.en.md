## What it is

cca-p-dojo is a static study site for Anthropic's Claude Certified Architect – Professional certification. It works offline.
It turns the 7 domains and 38 task statements in the official Exam Guide into structured notes and pairs them with 190 original scenario questions.
Every question is tied to the note section it tests, so a wrong answer takes you straight back to the material.

## Not affiliated with Anthropic

This project is **not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic**. It is a community-made study tool. Claude, Anthropic, and the related certification names are trademarks of Anthropic PBC.

None of the questions are real exam items or recollections of them. Mock exam scores are estimates: the site scales linearly (correct ÷ total × 1000), and the official scaled score may be calculated differently. Treat the number as a rough readiness check.

## Features

| Module | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Study notes** | 38 sections, one per task statement, plus one finished appendix, R.1 (distractor types). More appendices are planned. Each section covers what the objective tests, core concepts, decision rules, common traps, and question cues, and it marks which points are official guidance and which are rules of thumb. |
| **Practice** | You see the explanation as soon as you answer, including why each wrong option fails, with a link back to the matching note. Filter by domain, by section, unseen questions only, weakest first, or bookmarks. |
| **Missed questions** | Wrong answers are collected automatically and grouped by domain. A question leaves the list after two correct answers in a row. |
| **Mock exam** | 63 questions on a 120-minute timer, drawn by official domain weight (11 / 8 / 12 / 10 / 9 / 9 / 4 questions for D1 through D7). There is no feedback during the exam. When you submit, you get an estimated score out of 1000, a per-domain breakdown, and a question-by-question review. |
| **Advanced drills (optional)** | "Commit before you look" shows only the stem until you write down your reasoning. "Judgement of degree" leaves just the correct answer and its closest distractor. |
| **Import and export** | Progress lives in your browser's `localStorage` (key `ccap.v1`). "Manage progress" in the footer lets you download or copy it, and import it from a file, by drag and drop, or by pasting. Before importing, you see the two versions side by side and choose whether to merge or replace. |
| **Bilingual** | Switch languages from the top bar. The interface, the notes, and all 190 questions (stems, options, explanations, and wrong-option notes) are available in Chinese and English. |

## Originality and sources

- The notes and all 190 questions were written for this project. **No text was copied from third-party prep repositories, and no official sample questions were adapted or included.** On the author's machine, `scripts/check-sample-overlap.js` compares the notes and English questions against a local copy of the official sample questions using four-word fragments and requires zero matches. That copy is never committed.
- Facts that change over time, such as models, thinking behavior, and pricing, follow the Anthropic documentation as of the day they were written, and the notes give the date checked. The official URLs behind each section, and what each one was used to confirm, are listed in [`docs/sources.md`](docs/sources.md).
- Anything labelled a rule of thumb is engineering judgement, not an official Anthropic requirement.

## Run it locally

There are no dependencies and no build step, and you don't need Node to use the site. From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 4321
```

Then open <http://localhost:4321/>; the landing page redirects to `cca-p/`.

## Checks

Everything under `scripts/` is a dependency-free Node script, and a non-zero exit code means failure. After any change, run:

```bash
for f in scripts/*.js; do node "$f"; done
```

The checks cover zh/en string alignment, complete wrong-option explanations, blueprint quotas for the bank (`node scripts/check-blueprint.js --strict`), overlap with the official samples, and regression tests for resuming practice, switching languages, and guarding imports. A clean run means the format and rules hold. It doesn't mean the content is right; that still takes a manual check against the notes and official sources.
